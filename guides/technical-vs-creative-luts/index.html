Technical vs Creative LUTs: Which Comes First?

A technical LUT maps a defined input to a defined output. A creative LUT adds a look. Convert first only when the look expects the converted image. A hybrid LUT can do both, so stacking another conversion may give the wrong result.

Two jobs, different inputs

Technical: change the encoding

Example: Apple Log to Rec.709. The expected camera curve and gamut matter. A Sony S-Log LUT does not become an Apple Log transform just because both images look flat.

Creative: choose the appearance

A look may alter contrast, hues or saturation. It still expects a specific input. “Cinematic” is a style label, not an input color space.

Read the LUT’s input before choosing an order

For a look designed for Rec.709, the simple chain is Apple Log → matching conversion → Rec.709 look. A look designed for Log belongs at a different stage. Check its documentation instead of assuming every creative LUT comes last.

Why can two LUTs look worse?

Some LUTs combine normalization and a look. Feeding that hybrid LUT an already-converted image can exaggerate contrast or clip detail. An editor may also apply camera conversion automatically. Check both before adding another transform.
