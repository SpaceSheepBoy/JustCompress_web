Does Changing 72 DPI to 300 DPI Make a Photo Sharper?

Changing a photo from 72 DPI to 300 DPI without resampling changes its nominal print size, not its captured detail or pixel count. A sharper-looking label is not a sharper photograph.

Choose the approach

Pixels and print size are linked

A 1800 × 1200 pixel image at 300 pixels per inch describes a 6 × 4 inch print. The same pixels at 72 pixels per inch describe 25 × about 16.7 inches. Neither metadata setting adds information to the image.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Open the photo in DPI: Photo Print Prep and inspect its pixel dimensions. Decide whether you need a DPI-only change or a specific print size.
  2. For a metadata-only adjustment, keep pixels and the relevant format, color and location settings unchanged. Basic single-photo DPI changes are free.
  3. Read back the exported pixels, both DPI axes and physical size. Confirm the print service’s requirements and scaling settings before ordering.

Resampling is a different operation

Resampling adds or removes pixels. Enlarging can make a grid larger without recreating fine detail that the camera never captured. Cropping also changes the pixels available for the final print, so inspect composition and size together.

Use the correct feature

Space Sheep Studio develops our iPhone print-preparation app. Advanced print-size fitting offers three successful free exports before Pro; basic DPI edits never use that allowance. DPI-only edits preserve image data when format, color and location are kept. Resizing or conversion can re-save the image, and re-saved files do not preserve HDR gain maps. Keep the original and inspect a print proof for important work.
