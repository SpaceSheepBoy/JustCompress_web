Why Can Log Footage Look Noisier After Rec.709 Conversion?

Log footage can look noisier after Rec.709 conversion because the transform changes contrast and makes existing shadow noise easier to see. Conversion does not establish that the source was clean, and a LUT is not a denoiser.

Choose the approach

Start with the decision

A flat Log preview compresses the visible contrast of a scene. Normalizing that preview can reveal weak shadows, compression artifacts or an unsuitable exposure. A mismatched camera transform can create additional problems, so check the source profile before diagnosing the recording.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Compare the same frame at the same display size. Avoid judging one zoomed screenshot against another player’s fitted preview.
  2. Confirm the camera’s Log curve and gamut. Check whether your editor has already normalized the footage before adding a LUT.
  3. For supported Apple ProRes Log footage, create a short Rec.709 copy with VideoFix and examine shadows during playback. Retain the source for any separate grading or noise-reduction work.

Look at motion too

Noise changes between frames; a still can exaggerate or hide how distracting it is. Inspect a dark, relatively uniform area and a moving subject. Do not copy exposure numbers from another camera: appropriate capture settings depend on the camera, profile and scene. Sony’s technical discussion illustrates why exposure and viewing transforms are separate decisions.
