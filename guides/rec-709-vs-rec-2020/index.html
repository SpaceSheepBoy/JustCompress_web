Rec.709 vs Rec.2020: Color Gamut Is Not HDR

Rec.2020 defines a wider color gamut than Rec.709. That means it can represent a broader palette of colors, not that every Rec.2020 video is HDR. Choose an export for the receiving workflow, and check both its color primaries and transfer function.

Separate gamut from brightness

A bigger palette, not more pixels

Rec.709 is the standard-gamut HDTV reference. Rec.2020 uses wider-gamut red, green and blue primaries. These color definitions are separate from your export resolution: selecting 4K alone does not tell you how its colors are encoded.

Why Rec.2020 does not automatically mean HDR

Color gamut describes the palette; dynamic range describes the span of brightness. Rec.2020 primaries also appear in Rec.2100 HDR systems using PQ or HLG transfer functions. A primaries label alone is not enough to identify the brightness encoding.

Which should you export?

Use Rec.709 SDR when the recipient or editor requests standard SDR delivery. Keep a supported wide-gamut HDR workflow when HDR delivery is required. Converting to a smaller gamut can change colors outside that gamut. Changing a metadata label is not a substitute for color conversion, and exporting Rec.709 footage as Rec.2020 cannot recover colors never captured.
