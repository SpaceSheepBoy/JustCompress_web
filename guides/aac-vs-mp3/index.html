AAC vs MP3: Which Audio Format Should You Choose?

Choose AAC when the receiving app or device supports it and efficient encoding matters. Choose MP3 when a recipient or older player specifically needs it. Both are lossy formats: keep your original, and judge a short export by listening rather than its extension.

Choose for the destination

AAC: efficient encoding

AAC can preserve slightly more quality than MP3 at a similar file size, depending on the encoder and material. It is a useful listening-copy choice for compatible devices.

MP3: broad compatibility

Use MP3 when the destination requests .mp3 or you need a copy for a player that does not accept AAC. Check the actual receiving requirements first.

Will AAC always make a smaller file?

No. Duration and total bitrate determine most of the encoded size. Ten minutes at a constant 128 kbps is about 9.6 MB before headers and metadata, whichever codec you choose. AAC efficiency is a quality tradeoff, not an automatic size reduction. For the calculation, see our audio bitrate guide below.

Keep the original before converting

Converting AAC to MP3, or MP3 to AAC, cannot recover discarded detail. Another lossy encode can reduce quality. Export from the best available original and listen to speech, music and quiet passages in the copy before sharing.

A desktop option

On Mac, Apple Music can convert eligible local files with its AAC or MP3 encoder. That is an alternative if the files are already on your computer. Apple limits conversion of iTunes Store purchases to iTunes Plus songs.
