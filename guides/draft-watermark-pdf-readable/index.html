PDF Watermark Opacity: Keep the Mark and Text Readable

Use a light, readable DRAFT watermark on a separate PDF copy, then inspect every page. The mark communicates that the document is unfinished; it does not prevent copying, editing or redistribution.

Choose the approach

Make status clear

Use a short status word rather than a paragraph of instructions. A mark that obscures figures or footnotes makes review harder and can defeat the purpose of sending the draft.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Open the PDF from Files in PDF Watermark & Stamp and enter DRAFT as the watermark text.
  2. Choose repeated diagonal marks or one centered mark. Adjust opacity, size and color while previewing both dense and sparse pages.
  3. Export a new PDF. Open it outside the app, inspect every page and give the copy a filename that identifies its draft status.

Opacity needs a page check

There is no universal opacity that works for every PDF. A mark over a dark photograph may be hard to see; the same mark over small black text may interfere with reading. Inspect tables, totals, signatures and fine print. Keep the unmarked source for your own editing and future final versions. If the copy will be printed, test one page on the target printer: a light screen preview does not guarantee the same printed appearance.

Visible marking has limits

The current iPhone app creates text watermarks on device and leaves the original untouched. It does not turn a watermark into encryption, redaction or a digital signature. Ask the recipient whether a marked copy is acceptable when their submission rules matter. Space Sheep Studio develops this app; exports require Pro. This guide covers document labeling rather than legal protection.
