Is Apple Log HDR? Separate the Recording from Delivery

Apple Log is a recording encoding for later color processing; HDR is also a delivery choice. Log footage can be processed for an SDR or HDR output. A clip tagged HLG is not automatically Apple Log.

Choose the approach

Make the first check

A pale preview is a symptom, not an identification method. Applying an Apple Log transform to ordinary HLG footage because both look unfamiliar can introduce another color error.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Check the camera recording setting and the original file’s color information in a trusted editor. Keep the original tags. A MOV extension or ProRes label alone does not identify the transfer function.
  2. For verified Apple ProRes Log that needs standard SDR delivery, use the supported Log-to-Rec.709 conversion in VideoFix: HDR Video Converter. For HDR input, use its HDR-to-SDR workflow instead.
  3. Inspect the saved H.264 MP4 in the intended SDR player. Check faces, highlights and shadows. Do not apply the same Log conversion again to an already converted Rec.709 copy.

Recording and viewing are separate decisions

Apple’s Final Cut Camera guide separates standard, HDR, Log and RAW workflows. Log preserves a different starting representation for color work; the editor’s output settings determine the delivery. A normal-looking, color-managed preview does not prove that the source has already been permanently converted.

Match the supported source

Space Sheep Studio develops VideoFix for iPhone. Its public listing supports Apple ProRes Log and HDR conversion into Rec.709 SDR, with three free conversions before a purchase is needed. It does not establish Apple Log 2, HEVC Log, arbitrary camera profiles or HDR output here.

Limits and final checks

If source identification is uncertain, resolve it before batch conversion. Keep the camera original when you may want a different grade or HDR delivery later.
