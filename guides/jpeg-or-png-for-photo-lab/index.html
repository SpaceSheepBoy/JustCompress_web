JPEG or PNG for a Photo Lab Upload?

Start with the formats your photo lab accepts. If the lab asks for JPEG, export one JPEG from the best source you have and check its pixel dimensions after cropping.

Quick check

Check format and dimensions separately

A PNG can preserve its encoded pixels without lossy compression. It cannot add detail to a small source or recover detail lost when a JPEG was first made. A large file is not proof of a sharp print.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Check the lab’s current upload rules for the book or print you want. Ask about accepted formats and recommended pixel dimensions for that product.
  2. In DPI: Photo Print Prep, set your target paper size and density. Review the crop and check whether the pixels remaining after the crop meet the lab’s recommendation.
  3. Export the prepared file. Confirm its format and read back its actual dimensions. Upload the file the lab needs and inspect the lab’s crop preview before placing the order.

Choose one output from the best source

If you already have an accepted JPEG of sufficient size, you can often use it as it is. If you need a new crop or a format the lab accepts, start from the best original available. Keep that original for future exports.
