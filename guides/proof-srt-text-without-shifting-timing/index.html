Correct SRT Text Without Moving the Caption Timing

Keep the video cut the same and change only the words that need correction. Compare the old and new cue times before you send the revised SRT.

Quick check

Treat the words and cue times separately

An SRT cue has a number, a start and end time, and subtitle text. A blank line separates it from the next cue. A spelling correction usually needs a text edit only.

Try it on iPhone

  1. If the captions were made in Subtitle Maker: Just Captions, open the same video with its captions and edit the transcript before exporting a new SRT. Keep the first export to compare.
  2. Open both SRT files and compare the start and end values for the changed cues. Play them against the same video to check that the words still appear at the intended time.
  3. If you only have a standalone SRT, use a plain-text editor on a copy. Change the caption text and leave the numbers, timestamp lines and blank lines in place.

Avoid re-creating the captions for a small edit

Generating the transcript again can introduce new cue boundaries. Make a local correction when the existing timing is already usable. Keep a separate version if you also need to retime a line.

Proof the lines around the correction

A longer correction can take more time to read. Play the cue and its neighbors. Where the line becomes hard to read, review the wording and delivery requirements before you change its timing.

Common questions

Can I keep the timing on a different cut of the video?

Check against the new cut. Clips removed or inserted earlier can shift later cues out of sync.

What if I only have the SRT file?

Open a copy in a plain-text editor and leave the timestamp lines unchanged.
