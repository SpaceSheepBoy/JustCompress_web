Check Photo Resolution After an 8 × 10 Crop

Crop a portrait photo to 4:5 first, then divide the remaining pixels by 8 and 10 inches. At 300 pixels per inch, you need at least 2400 × 3000 pixels after the crop.

Quick check

Use the pixels left after cropping

For an 8 × 10 print that fills the paper, the image needs the same shape. A file can look large enough before cropping and still fall below the lab’s target after you trim the edges.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Check your photo lab’s target and whether it adds any trim. Open the original in DPI: Photo Print Prep and choose 8 × 10 inches with the intended orientation.
  2. Confirm the crop with room for the subject’s edges. Check the resulting pixel dimensions against the lab target. For landscape, swap the width and height.
  3. Export a copy and read back its dimensions. If you need more captured detail, try a less aggressive crop with borders, a smaller print, or a higher-resolution original.

Work through a near-match

A 3000 × 3736 portrait can be cropped to 2988 × 3735 pixels at 4:5. Divide 2988 by 8 and 3735 by 10. Both give 373.5 pixels per inch, before any further trim.

Check whether the lab crops again

A photo editor’s crop and a lab’s order preview can differ. Inspect both. Leave space near the edge where the lab warns that it may trim the print.

Common questions

Can resampling add captured detail?

Resampling creates pixels using existing data. Fine detail still depends on the source photo.

What if I need to keep all edges?

Use borders or choose a paper shape closer to the original.
