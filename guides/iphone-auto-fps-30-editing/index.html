iPhone Auto FPS in Low Light and 30 FPS Editing

If an iPhone clip shot in low light behaves differently in a 30 FPS edit, check the original clip’s frame rate. If the editor needs a steady 30 FPS file, test one converted copy before processing the rest.

Quick check

Check the clip you already have

On supported iPhones, Auto FPS can reduce the capture rate in low light. The camera setting you see today does not tell you the timing of a clip you recorded yesterday.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Check the clip’s video information and play a section with movement and clear speech. Keep the original as a reference.
  2. If your editor needs a steady 30 FPS input, select the 30 FPS frame-rate output in VideoFix: HDR Video Converter and export one copy.
  3. Import the copy into the same timeline. Check a moving subject and compare dialogue near the beginning and end. Check color if the source is HDR and the copy is SDR.

Decide if the extra export helps

If the original plays correctly and your editor handles it as expected, you may be able to edit it directly. A 30 FPS output can repeat or discard frames. It cannot add moments the camera missed, and fast movement can still look uneven.

Separate capture from delivery

For future recordings, review Auto FPS under Camera recording settings if you need more consistent capture. Try a short low-light test with your chosen setting.
