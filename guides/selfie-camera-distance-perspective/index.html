Selfie Proportions Look Different? Check Camera Distance

A selfie taken very close to your face can show different proportions from one taken farther away. Check camera distance before blaming screen-light color or looking for a face-correction filter.

Choose the approach

Start with the decision

Perspective depends on the camera’s position relative to the subject. At close range, features nearer the lens occupy more of the picture relative to features farther back. Lighting changes visibility and shadows; it does not move the camera viewpoint.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Start with a comfortable amount of room light. In GlowPop: Selfie Glow Camera, use the front-camera preview with a neutral screen-light color.
  2. Keep your head angle steady and move the phone modestly farther away. Reframe with your position, then adjust available brightness while checking comfort and facial highlights.
  3. Take a test photo and inspect the saved result. Compare framing and light separately. Keep whichever viewpoint suits your purpose, without treating one image as a verdict on how you look.

Distance creates a lighting tradeoff

Moving the phone farther away changes the viewpoint and usually reduces how much its screen illuminates the face. Add suitable room light rather than bringing the phone extremely close solely for brightness. Nikon’s lens guide explains the relationship between subject distance and perspective.
