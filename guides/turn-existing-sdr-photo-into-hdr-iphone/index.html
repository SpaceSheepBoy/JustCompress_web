Can You Turn an Old SDR Photo Into HDR on iPhone?

Yes. You can make an HDR copy from an SDR photo as a creative brightness effect. Any detail that was already clipped in the source stays missing.

Quick check

Choose a photo with useful highlight information

A photo with visible detail in a lamp shade or reflection is a useful test. If an area is already solid white in the original, expanding brightness cannot reconstruct what was there.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Keep the SDR original. In PhotoFix: HDR to Normal Photos, choose Normal photo to HDR and select the intended still-image format.
  2. Export a separate copy. View both files in a viewer and on a display that support HDR. Keep the display brightness and viewing conditions steady.
  3. Share a test copy to the actual destination before converting a collection. Save or send the SDR original as an alternative when the destination does not retain HDR.

Separate a brighter presentation from captured detail

HDR can give compatible displays more room to render highlights. The original still sets the limit on detail, including texture in bright areas and fine gradations. Compare the same area on the same display to judge whether you like the effect.

Check the delivery format and destination

The app supports HDR still exports in HEIC, JPEG and PNG. Viewer support varies, so a normal-looking preview does not prove the copy has lost its HDR information. Check on a supported device before changing formats.

Common questions

Can I keep Live Photo movement in the HDR copy?

The app exports HDR as a still. Keep the original if you need its movement and sound.

Will every viewer show the same brightness?

Display capabilities and viewer settings affect how the file looks.
