HLG vs PQ: Two Ways to Encode HDR Video

HLG and PQ interpret brightness differently. Check the clip’s transfer function before choosing a color conversion, even if both files are labeled HDR.

Find the transfer function

Same HDR family, different interpretation

The ICC BT.2100 registry identifies both PQ and HLG transfer functions. A .mov or .mp4 extension does not tell you which system the picture uses.

Check a copy in the intended player

  1. Check the source color information in a trusted editor or media inspector; keep the original tags and file.
  2. If the recipient needs SDR, use VideoFix: HDR Video Converter to create a Rec.709 SDR sharing copy.
  3. Play the result in the intended player and inspect faces, bright lights and shadows before sending it.

Why switching a label is not conversion

HLG is designed around a relative scene-based signal, while PQ describes an absolute display-light relationship. Reading one as the other can produce an incorrect image. Changing a filename, selecting H.264 or attaching a color label does not by itself perform the required transformation. Follow the source interpretation and destination color pipeline.

When an SDR copy is useful

Common questions

If my inspector only says BT.2020, have I identified HLG?

No. That may identify the color primaries. Look for a separate transfer field or the original recording settings before choosing HLG or PQ.

Do I need an SDR copy when the recipient already supports HDR?

If the original opens and displays correctly in the receiving workflow, keep it for HDR delivery. Make an SDR copy separately when another destination requires it.
