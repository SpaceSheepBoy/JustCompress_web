Why HDR-to-SDR Needs Tone Mapping, Not Just a New Label

HDR-to-SDR conversion needs tone mapping because an SDR output cannot represent the same highlight range as an HDR source. A new codec, file extension or color label alone does not fit those highlights into the destination range.

Choose the approach

Map, then check

Tone mapping redistributes brightness into the output range. Clipping simply cuts values beyond a boundary, potentially making distinct bright details look the same. Neither method can recover texture already missing from the source.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Keep the tagged HDR original. Identify the intended destination: use an SDR copy when the recipient requests standard Rec.709 SDR.
  2. In VideoFix: HDR Video Converter on iPhone, choose Convert HDR to SDR and export a separate H.264 MP4.
  3. Review faces, bright windows and shadow transitions in the saved copy and in the intended player. Retain the HDR original for future work.

Why the result can look different

The conversion must decide how to represent highlights while keeping the main subject useful. Different tone-mapping pipelines can produce different contrast and highlight roll-off. A webpage illustration cannot demonstrate an HDR display’s real brightness or establish the quality of an App export.
