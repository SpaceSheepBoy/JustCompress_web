Check the display and receiving app

A bright PNG can be valid

An unexpectedly bright PNG is not necessarily a display fault. The current PNG specification includes color signaling and HDR-related metadata. The image, viewing app and display must work together to determine its appearance.

Choose and test your PNG export

  1. Decide whether you need a standard SDR sharing copy or an intentional HDR image. Check the destination’s requirements instead of selecting PNG solely because you associate it with screenshots.
  2. In PhotoFix: HDR to Normal Photos, choose HDR to normal photo for SDR delivery, then select PNG when you need that format or transparency. Keep the original.
  3. Inspect the saved file in the intended app and on the target display when possible. If you choose a creative HDR export instead, verify its appearance on a compatible HDR display.

Three properties can vary independently

PNG’s lossless compression describes how its encoded image data is stored. Transparency describes whether pixels can reveal a background. Dynamic range describes a different aspect of the represented light.

Keep transparency separate from brightness

Preserving transparency does not make an HDR source into SDR, and lossless compression does not mean a color conversion is reversible.

Common questions

If a PNG keeps transparency, is it also HDR?

Transparency controls which parts show a background. HDR describes light representation. Either property can be checked independently.

Why does the same PNG look less bright in another viewer?

The viewer or display may render its brightness differently. Test the intended destination, and make a separate SDR copy if that is what it needs.
