Convert H.264 to HEVC on iPhone: Check Size and Playback

Try an HEVC export when an H.264 video takes too much storage, then compare its actual bytes and playback compatibility. HEVC can be efficient, but re-encoding does not guarantee a smaller file at every setting or identical quality.

Choose the approach

Keep the original until checked

H.264 and HEVC are codecs; MP4 is a container that can carry either. A .mp4 filename therefore does not prove which codec is inside or whether an older player will decode it.

Try it on iPhone

  1. In Phone Cleaner: Photo & Video, choose the H.264 source and an MP4/HEVC export. Export tools require Pro.
  2. Choose a suitable quality preset and export a separate copy. Compare the original and output file sizes in the app.
  3. Play the copy through the important motion and fine detail, then test the actual device or app that must receive it.

Smaller has conditions

Codec efficiency depends on scene content, resolution and export quality. A high-quality HEVC output can exceed a low-bitrate H.264 original, while repeated conversion may add artifacts. Return to the source if you need to try another setting. Do not delete the original merely because an export completed.

Compatibility can outweigh storage

Choose H.264 for recipients that need wider playback support. Our iPhone app supports both H.264-to-HEVC and the reverse direction, along with local compression and file-size comparison. Space Sheep Studio develops Phone Cleaner: Photo & Video. It does not promise fixed percentage savings or a lossless codec change. Check that the saved copy is useful before deciding which version belongs in your archive.
