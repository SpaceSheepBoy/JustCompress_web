A Life Grid Shows a Chosen Timeline, Not Your Expiry Date

A life grid visualizes time against the birthday and lifespan setting you choose. Its empty squares are part of that chosen timeline, not a prediction of how long you will live.

Choose the approach

Start with the decision

The same birthday can produce different remaining space when the chosen lifespan changes. That does not mean your health or future changed. The display is a way to reflect on priorities, and its assumptions should stay visible in how you interpret it.

Try it on iPhone

  1. In Dying Soon: Screen Time Limit, review your birthday and selected lifespan before reading the life grid.
  2. Choose a years, months or weeks view that helps you reflect. Treat the remaining space as a planning perspective rather than an individual forecast.
  3. Pick one concrete activity you want to make room for today. If useful, start a thirty-minute Block Now session for distracting apps while you do it.

Do not turn the grid into a score

A filled square does not judge whether a week was valuable, productive or wasted. A timeline cannot account for everything that happened in it. You can use a coarser view or set it aside if the display is unhelpful. Optional reminders are a separate choice; there is no need to enable notifications to use the app.

What the app actually offers

Space Sheep Studio develops Dying Soon for iPhone. It combines the life grid with Screen Time app blocking and optional reminders. The current listing explicitly describes the countdown as a reflection tool, not a medical assessment or a death prediction. One scheduled rule and unlimited thirty-minute Block Now sessions are free; Pro adds more scheduled rules.
