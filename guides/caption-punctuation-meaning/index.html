Auto Captions Need Punctuation: Check the Meaning First

Punctuate auto captions according to the spoken meaning, rather than adding a full stop at every caption break. Correct sentence boundaries can change what viewers understand even when every recognized word is spelled correctly.

Choose the approach

Start with the decision

“Let’s eat, Sam” addresses Sam; “Let’s eat Sam” says something different. The example is deliberately simple, but it shows why punctuation review belongs beside spelling review. A caption card ending does not necessarily mean that the speaker finished a sentence.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Generate captions in Subtitle Maker: Just Captions, then listen to a complete sentence while reading the transcript.
  2. Edit punctuation and capitalization to match the thought. If you choose optional AI Correct, review its suggestions against the audio before keeping them.
  3. Preview and export the captioned video. Watch the saved copy at phone size and check that sentence endings, questions and quotations still read naturally.

Keep the words faithful

Do not change a speaker’s intended meaning just to make a sentence look tidier. Hesitation and informal speech may need careful judgment. Section508.gov identifies grammar, punctuation and awkward line breaks as common auto-caption problems and recommends editing the result. This small review does not by itself certify a video as accessible.
