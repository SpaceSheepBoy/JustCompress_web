4 × 6 Photo at 300 DPI: Start with 1200 × 1800 Pixels

A 4 × 6 inch photo needs 1200 × 1800 pixels at 300 pixels per inch. Use the dimensions after cropping to check whether your photo meets that target.

Check shape as well as pixels

A 4 × 6 print has a 2:3 aspect ratio. A differently shaped original needs cropping or borders. Inspect faces near the edges before accepting a crop.

Prepare a 4 × 6 file

  1. In DPI: Photo Print Prep, choose a 4 × 6 inch target and 300 DPI; decide whether you need white borders.
  2. Review the crop and pixel sufficiency. Keep pixels, lower the target DPI or resample according to the print requirement.
  3. Export and read back the actual dimensions and both DPI axes. Inspect the file and confirm the print service’s scaling settings.

Work the numbers

4 × 300 = 1200 and 6 × 300 = 1800. The same 1200 × 1800 pixels at 200 pixels per inch describe a 6 × 9 inch image. The pixel data has not gained detail merely because the physical-size metadata changed. The app uses DPI terminology; image sampling is more precisely pixels per inch.

Proof before a large order

Our iPhone app supports print-size fitting, crop confirmation and export verification, plus a 4 × 6 PDF proof with a 100 mm ruler. Print the proof at 100%; printer settings can still change the result. Basic single-photo DPI edits are free. Print-size fitting is an advanced export, with three successful advanced exports free before Pro. Resampling enlarges the pixel grid but cannot recreate missing captured detail.

Common questions

Does a 1200 × 1800 file have to be vertical?

No. A landscape file can be 1800 × 1200 pixels. Match the orientation to the paper and preview the crop before exporting.

What if my photo is smaller than 1200 × 1800?

Ask the lab what it accepts and inspect a proof. You can print at a lower pixel density or choose a smaller size. Resampling will not add captured detail.
