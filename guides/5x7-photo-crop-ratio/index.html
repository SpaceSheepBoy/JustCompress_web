Why a 4 × 6 Photo Crops Differently at 5 × 7

A 4 × 6 photo has a 2:3 aspect ratio, while a 5 × 7 print has a 5:7 ratio. Enlarging one directly into the other requires cropping or borders. Decide which edges matter before approving the print.

Choose the approach

Shape comes before DPI

At 300 pixels per inch, a 5 × 7 image needs 1500 × 2100 pixels. Those numbers describe the final grid, not permission to stretch a 1200 × 1800 picture into a different shape. Stretching distorts proportions.

Try it on iPhone

  1. In DPI: Photo Print Prep, set a 5 × 7 inch target, choose the intended DPI and decide whether white borders are acceptable.
  2. Review the independent crop. Keep faces and important edges inside the frame, then check pixel sufficiency.
  3. Export and read back actual pixel dimensions, both DPI axes and physical size. Confirm the print service’s fit/fill settings.

Crop or preserve all edges

Filling the entire 5 × 7 area from a 2:3 source removes some content. Keeping the complete source means unused space or borders. Neither option is universally right: a head near an edge favors preserving composition, while a borderless frame may favor a carefully approved crop.
