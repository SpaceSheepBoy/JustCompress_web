Use Colored Screen Light for a Deliberate Selfie Look

For a deliberate colored selfie, light your face with the chosen color before taking the photo. Keep room light controlled so that it does not wash out the tint you are trying to create.

Choose the approach

Make the first check

This is a creative lighting choice. A blue or pink face is not intended to reproduce neutral skin color, and a single phone screen does not promise the two-sided look of a studio with multiple lights.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Open the front-camera preview in GlowPop: Selfie Glow Camera. Choose one screen-light color and use a comfortable brightness.
  2. Keep the framing steady while adjusting nearby lamps or your position. Compare the tint with and without strong competing room light. Leave enough light to see facial detail.
  3. Take a test photo and inspect the saved result. Adjust the color or brightness if highlights lose detail or the tint overwhelms the expression you want.

Light and a filter do different work

Colored light falls on surfaces before capture, so face shape, nearby objects and shadows influence the result. A later color filter changes an already captured image. Nikon’s lighting lesson demonstrates color control with physical lights and gels; it does not establish that a phone screen has the output or precision of studio equipment.

Keep the experiment simple

Limits and final checks

No split-color mode, calibrated output or white-balance lock is claimed here. Ambient light, phone distance and camera processing all matter. Use the saved photo as your check, and choose a different setup if the screen alone cannot produce the look comfortably.
