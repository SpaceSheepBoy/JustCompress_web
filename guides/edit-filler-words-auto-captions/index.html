Edit Filler Words in Auto Captions?

Keep filler words when they carry meaning, show uncertainty or are required by the caption delivery rules. Remove a filler if the transcript added a word the speaker never said.

Quick check

Listen before you delete

“I might, um, need more time” can express hesitation. Cutting it to “I need more time” also removes uncertainty. Keep the words that affect what the speaker meant.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Generate captions in Subtitle Maker: Just Captions. Review the complete sentence with sound on, including the seconds before and after the filler.
  2. Open the transcript editor and correct any false words. For captions that must be verbatim, keep the spoken fillers. If the project allows light editing, use the same approach throughout and preserve intent and tone.
  3. Preview the result with sound on to check timing. Watch it once with sound off to check whether the line still makes sense, then export the captioned copy.

Match the project’s caption rules

A casual, self-produced clip may allow light edits. An interview, quotation, classroom recording or commissioned project may call for a closer record of the speech. Ask for the delivery rules before tidying up someone else’s words.
