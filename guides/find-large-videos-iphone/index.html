Find Large iPhone Videos Before Choosing What to Compress

Find the large videos first, then decide which are worth compressing. A long clip is not always the largest: duration, resolution and encoding settings all affect file size.

Choose the approach

Make the first check

Start in Settings, General, iPhone Storage to see whether Photos is a major contributor. The device’s recommendations can vary. Avoid deleting unrelated apps if the main problem is a small group of large recordings.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Open Storage Clean in Phone Cleaner: Photo & Video and review the large videos it finds. Identify the event, duration and visible contents before choosing a candidate.
  2. For a video you want to keep, create a compressed sharing copy with suitable settings. Keep the original while you compare the saved file sizes.
  3. Play the output from beginning to end, or inspect representative sections in a long recording. Check sound, orientation and important detail before deciding what to retain.

File review comes before deletion

A large file may be an important original rather than waste. Several unrelated large videos are also a different problem from duplicate copies. Make a short review list and work through it; do not assume that the first large result is safe to remove.

Check what actually changes storage

Space Sheep Studio develops this iPhone app. Its public listing includes large-media discovery, local compression and original-versus-export size comparison; Pro unlocks export tools. Saving a smaller copy adds another file until you make a separate retention decision.

Limits and final checks

If iCloud Photos is enabled, Apple warns that deleting a photo or video also deletes it from other devices using that library. Keep a verified independent copy when needed. The app does not promise a particular number of gigabytes reclaimed or automatic safe deletion of every large recording.
