Make a Simple Room Mood Board from Photos on iPhone

A simple room mood board can be one image containing your room photo, furniture references and material samples. Use it to compare a visual direction, then check physical size and real samples separately.

Choose the approach

Make the first check

Start with one room and one decision, such as whether a rug works with an existing sofa. A board full of unrelated rooms can hide the choice you actually need to make.

Try it on iPhone

  1. Collect your own reference photos or images you have permission to use. Include the existing room, one main furniture piece and a few relevant fabric, wood or paint samples.
  2. Import the references into Combine Photos: Stitch & Grid. Choose a layout, position each image and adjust zoom so the material or furniture feature remains visible.
  3. Export a JPEG or PNG and view the complete board at phone size. Remove weak references if the important details become too small. Share the exported image when asking someone to compare the direction.

Treat the board as a shortlist

Photograph physical samples under similar light when practical. Supplier photos, room lighting and display settings can change how a color appears. Keep product links, dimensions and prices in a separate note; the collage itself is not a shopping database or proof that furniture fits.

Know the layout boundary

Space Sheep Studio develops our iPhone collage app. It combines 2 to 30 photos or screenshots using 11 layouts and exports images up to 3000 pixels wide. One export is free; further exports require Pro.

Limits and final checks

This workflow uses a photo grid, with no claim of background removal, text editing or a realistic room simulation. Before buying, measure the room and inspect material samples in the actual space. A mood board helps organize choices; it cannot certify paint matching or spatial clearance.
