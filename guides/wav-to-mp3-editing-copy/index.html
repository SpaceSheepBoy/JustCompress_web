WAV vs MP3: Which Copy Should You Keep for Editing?

Keep the WAV master for editing and make an MP3 copy for sharing when the recipient accepts it. MP3 discards audio information to save space; converting that MP3 back to WAV does not restore the discarded information.

A larger WAV file can be a useful editing source, while a smaller MP3 is convenient for delivery. The filename alone does not establish recording quality: a WAV made from an MP3 still contains the earlier losses.

Avoid repeated lossy exports

If you need to trim or process a recording again, return to the best available source. Repeated MP3 exports can accumulate artifacts. A higher bitrate can reduce audible losses, but it cannot guarantee transparency for every recording or recreate missing frequencies.

A practical archive habit

Name the files so that the editable master and delivery copy cannot be confused. Keep both until the recipient has checked the result.
