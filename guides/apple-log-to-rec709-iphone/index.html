Why Apple Log looks flat

Log footage is intended for a color-managed editing workflow. Low contrast in an ungraded Log preview does not by itself mean that the recording failed. If you want a standard-color copy for sharing rather than a full grading session, VideoFix supports converting Apple ProRes Log to Rec.709 SDR.

Keep a grading original

Converting to H.264 MP4 gives you a practical sharing copy. Keep the original ProRes Log footage for later color work. A standard SDR export is not a replacement for the original’s editing flexibility, and this workflow is not a claim that an automatic conversion can reproduce a particular cinematic grade.

Avoid applying the transform twice

Check how the destination editor treats the exported copy. A file already converted to standard Rec.709 should not receive the same Log-to-Rec.709 conversion again. If the output looks unusually contrasty, inspect the editor’s input-color settings and any applied LUTs before exporting again.

Check bright areas and skin tones

Review a section containing faces, bright light and shadow. Compare on the same display, then test the destination where you will share it. VideoFix processes locally, retains the original and offers three free conversions before a paid plan is required.

Common questions

Is Apple Log the same as HDR?

No. Log encoding and HDR delivery are different concepts. Confirm the source and desired output color space.

Does this export keep the ProRes codec?

This workflow creates an H.264 MP4 sharing copy. Keep the original for ProRes editing.
