Useful answers

Before you start.

Is Apple Log the same as HDR?

No. Log footage and HDR video are different sources. VideoFix can convert supported Apple ProRes Log footage or HDR video to standard Rec.709 SDR color. The comparison on this page is an Apple Log example.

Can I convert HEVC that is already SDR?

Yes. Choose Convert HDR to SDR for an H.264 MP4 compatibility copy, including when the HEVC source is already SDR.

Will the copy look exactly like my original?

Conversion re-encodes the video, and HDR-to-SDR changes the brightness and color range. Keep your original and check color, sound and playback in the intended destination.

Can it fix every editing or playback problem?

No. A compatible codec or constant frame rate can help an appropriate workflow, but it cannot repair every existing synchronization problem. Device and destination requirements still apply.

Can I save only the sound?

Yes. Extract Audio saves the supported first audio track as an AAC M4A file.

What is included after the free conversions?

After three successful free conversions, Lifetime or Monthly Pro unlocks unlimited conversions. Lifetime is one payment; Monthly renews until you cancel it. Restore purchases with the Apple Account used for the purchase.

Why does my video look washed out after sharing?

A destination may interpret HDR or Log differently from the app where you filmed it. First check the source color information and compare playback in the intended destination. If that workflow needs standard SDR, make a Rec.709 copy; changing the filename to MP4 alone does not perform color conversion.

Is MP4 the same thing as H.264?

No. MP4 is a container, while H.264 and HEVC are video codecs. A video can already be an MP4 and still use a codec your destination does not accept. Choose the output codec and color handling for the destination rather than relying on the file extension.

Do I need to convert every iPhone video?

No. If the destination already accepts the source and it looks correct, keep using it. A separate conversion is useful when you need standard SDR color, H.264 compatibility, a selected frame rate or a different output resolution.

Can I change the frame rate for an editing project?

Yes. The frame-rate tool offers steady 24, 25, 30 and 60 FPS choices. Match the requirement of your project, then check movement and sound in the exported clip. A different frame rate cannot recover motion that was never captured.

Can I process a folder of clips in one go?

You can select several videos and apply a shared conversion goal. Review each finished copy because source clips can differ in color, resolution and frame rate. Keep enough free storage for both your originals and the outputs.

Will conversion make every video smaller?

No. Output size depends on duration, resolution, frame rate and encoding settings. H.264 can be larger than an efficient HEVC source. Use the optional size limit when appropriate and inspect the actual output size and quality afterwards.

Will my original footage be overwritten?

The workflow creates a separate converted copy. Keep your original until you have checked the export and any backups you need. Deleting source footage is a separate decision; a successful conversion is not a backup.

What should I check before sending the result?

Open the saved file, play the beginning and end, listen to the sound and inspect color in the app or device that will receive it. Check orientation, duration and file size as well as whether the video opens.

Should I use SDR for a project that needs HDR?

Keep the HDR source for an HDR delivery workflow. SDR conversion makes a different version for a standard-color destination; it is not an upgrade to the original. Check the delivery specification before choosing the output.

Where can I get help with a particular clip?

Use the VideoFix support page and describe the source format, intended output and where playback fails. Include the app and iOS versions and the steps you tried. You do not need to send private footage just to describe the issue.
