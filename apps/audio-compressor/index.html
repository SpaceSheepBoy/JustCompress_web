Useful answers

Before you start.

Can I convert an iPhone M4A recording to MP3?

Yes. Import the recording into Convert, choose MP3 and select the quality before exporting. Listen to the saved file and check that your destination accepts it.

Does a smaller file always sound the same?

No. Lower-bitrate or lossy exports can reduce sound quality. Keep the original, choose settings for your use and listen to the result before sending it.

Is this audio compression the same as making quiet voices louder?

No. File compression reduces encoded file size. It is different from dynamic-range compression or repairing the volume of a recording.

Can I save the audio from a video?

Yes. The app includes a video-to-audio extraction tool. Select your video, extract its audio, then save and listen to the result.

Can I use it for music practice?

Pitch, tempo and metronome tools are available alongside the file tools. Choose the tool for the practice task you want to do.

What is free and what needs Pro?

One successful export is included, while recording and playback remain free. Further exports and Pro tools require an eligible Lifetime purchase or active Monthly subscription. Failed or cancelled exports do not consume the included export.

How do I bring a Voice Memo into the app?

In Voice Memos, share the recording and save a copy to Files. Open the appropriate tool in Audio Compressor and choose that file. Keep the source recording while you check your exported clip.

Should I trim before compressing?

If you only need part of the recording, trim first so the export contains the useful section. Reducing duration can reduce file size without lowering the encoding quality of the kept section. Listen at both cut points before sharing.

How large will an MP3 export be?

For a constant bitrate, approximate size in decimal MB is duration in seconds multiplied by bitrate in kilobits per second, divided by 8,000. The calculator on this page estimates the audio payload. Metadata, encoder behavior and variable bitrate can change the final file.

Which bitrate should I choose?

Choose a setting you can actually listen to before committing. Speech and music have different needs, and a lower bitrate may lose detail. A higher export bitrate cannot recover detail missing from a low-quality source.

Can I make a recording fit an email attachment limit?

Use the calculator to estimate a target bitrate, then try compression or split the recording into parts. Check the final exported size and the attachment limit of your email service. Estimates do not guarantee a file will be accepted.

Does splitting automatically transcribe or find topics?

The split workflow divides a recording into parts. It is not a transcript or a promise that every boundary matches a speaker, sentence or topic. Review the segment lengths and listen to the clips before sending them.

Does converting to MP3 improve the original sound?

No. MP3 is a lossy format. Converting can make a file convenient for a destination, but cannot restore missing detail or remove all noise. Keep the original for future editing.

Should I keep WAV or export a smaller format?

Keep a WAV source when you need it for later editing or delivery. A compressed copy may be more convenient for listening and sharing. Follow the format requirement of the person or app receiving the file.

What should I check after an export?

Play the saved output, check its duration and file size, and listen around trim or split boundaries. Open it in the intended destination too. Successful export and successful delivery are separate checks.

Does the web calculator process my recording?

No. It only calculates from the duration, bitrate and target size you enter. It does not load, analyze, upload or convert an audio file. Use the iPhone app for the actual audio workflow.
