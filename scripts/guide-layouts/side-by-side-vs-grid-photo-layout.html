Side-by-side or grid?
Choose what viewers compare.

Use an equal pair when the viewer needs to compare two images. Use a grid when the viewer needs to scan a collection. Then check that each image still has enough room at the final sharing size.

01. Start with the reading task.

Your goalStart withCheck
Compare two viewsAn equal pairSimilar subject scale and clear order
Show a small collectionAn even gridEach image still has a purpose
Share readable screenshotsFewer images or a vertical pairThe smallest text at viewing size

For a product comparison, keep both items at a similar scale. For a sequence, arrange images in a clear reading order. A decorative grid is less useful when the reader has to hunt for the one detail that changed.

A collage does not restore missing detail.

A larger export cannot recover detail absent from a small source image. Layout changes how much space each source gets.

02. Give each image enough pixels.

At the same output width, more columns make every cell narrower. In a 1200-pixel-wide canvas with no gaps, two equal columns receive 600 pixels each. Three receive 400. Gaps and margins reduce that further.

That is why a three-column grid may work for large subjects but fail for screenshots with small text. Try a vertical pair, reduce the image count, or send the originals separately when detail matters more than fitting everything into one picture.

Check the crop as well as the size. Filling a cell can hide the edge of a document or part of a subject. Leaving some empty space may be preferable to cutting off the information you want to show. A panoramic stitch and a collage are different tasks; placing two images next to each other does not align a continuous scene.

03. Check the actual image you will send.

  1. Prepare copies of the originals.

    Crop only when it improves the comparison, and keep the source images. The iPhone Photos editor can crop and straighten an individual image before you assemble it.

  2. Arrange, then inspect every cell.

    Check subject scale, image order, cut-off edges and any small text. Use fewer images if one important detail becomes too small.

  3. Open the saved copy.

    View it at the size the recipient will see. If their destination compresses images, inspect the received copy as well. Keep the originals available for close inspection.

Apple documents native photo editing and links to cropping. If you only need to show two originals, sending both separately is a free alternative to making a combined image.
